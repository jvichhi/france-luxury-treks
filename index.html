If staying fully within France and maximizing luxury comfort is the priority, the Luberon is the strongest match: a bookable luxury tier, gentle terrain, the cheapest and most reliable direct flight (Marseille), and a mature self-guided-plus-luggage market. The Mont Blanc half-circuit is the only option leaving France, and the priciest, but delivers the most dramatic high-alpine scenery and the easiest gateway flight (direct to Geneva). Verdon and Pyrenees are the most budget-friendly on trek cost but have thinner luxury lodging and, for the Pyrenees, no reliable direct flight from Montreal.

Complete Trip Checklist

Documents & Money

  • Canadian passport valid 6+ months past return date; no visa needed for stays under 90 days (Schengen)
  • Travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage — essential for the Mont Blanc and Pyrenees options given remote terrain
  • Currency: Euro for France and Italy; Swiss Francs if overnighting on the Swiss side of a Mont Blanc variant
  • Notify your bank/credit card of European travel dates

Connectivity

  • EU-wide eSIM (e.g. Airalo, Holafly) is simpler than roaming add-ons across 3 countries on the Mont Blanc route
  • Offline maps downloaded in advance (Komoot, Maps.me) as mountain refuge wifi is unreliable

Packing Essentials

  • Broken-in hiking boots (not new) — the single biggest factor in trip comfort
  • Layered clothing: alpine routes swing from single digits °C at dawn to 25°C+ midday
  • Rain shell and trekking poles, especially for Mont Blanc and Pyrenees options
  • Power adapter: Type E/F for France (also covers most of Italy); Type J needed only if overnighting in Switzerland
  • Sun protection (altitude intensifies UV) and a lightweight sleeping bag liner for any refuge nights

Booking Timeline

  • Flights: book 5–7 months ahead for the best summer economy fares from Montreal
  • Luxury/private hotel rooms: 9–12 months ahead, especially July–August
  • Restaurant reservations (Michelin-starred tables): 4–8 weeks ahead minimum
A short physical prep note: even the gentler Luberon route involves 5–8 hour walking days on consecutive days. Two to three months of regular walking/hiking with a loaded daypack beforehand will make a meaningful difference, especially for the Mont Blanc and Pyrenees options with their higher daily elevation gain.
