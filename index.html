Luxury Self-Guided Treks in France

Four regional options · 6–7 days · natural walking pace, not compressed

Four distinct regional itineraries, each walked at a normal, unhurried pace (10–16 km/day) rather than a compressed version of a longer trek. Two are set in the Alps, two in the south of France. Prices below are per person, self-guided, based on double/twin occupancy with luggage transfer included unless noted.

OPTION 3

Gorges du Verdon

"Europe's Grand Canyon" — turquoise river, vultures overhead, perched rim villages

Route: Castellane → La Palud-sur-Verdon → Sentier Martel → Point Sublime (6 days / 5 nights)

~71 kmTotal distance
~4,100 mTotal ascent

  • Day 4: Sentier Martel descent into the gorge, signature hike (~15 km, +700/-950m)

Standard packages use simple 2-star family-run hotels; 3-star upgrades available on request. Luggage transfer adds roughly €60–195 to the base price depending on group size. Best April–October.
OPTION 4

French Pyrenees

One self-contained week from the GR10, which crosses the Pyrenees coast-to-coast

~85 kmTotal distance (est.)
~5,000+ mTotal ascent (est.)
From €620Per person (up to €760, group-size dependent)
7 days6 nights
  • Structure: pick one of 8 mapped weekly sections (e.g. central Pyrenees week Cauterets–Luchon through the Néouvielle massif, or a western Basque-country week)
  • Style: guided or self-guided by section, luggage transfer where road access allows
  • Terrain: wilder and less crowded than Mont Blanc or Provence; luxury nights are best concentrated in valley towns (Cauterets, Luz-Saint-Sauveur, Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port) rather than mountain refuges
The most rugged option and the hardest to pair with consistent luxury lodging night after night, since much of the trail passes through remote high country. Distance/elevation figures are estimates — they vary significantly by which of the 8 weekly sections is chosen.

If staying fully within France and maximizing luxury comfort is the priority, the Luberon is the strongest match — it already has a bookable luxury tier (5-star to 3-star charming inns), gentle terrain, and a mature self-guided-plus-luggage-transfer market, at a mid-range price point. The Verdon and Pyrenees options are the most budget-friendly but have thinner luxury accommodation. The Mont Blanc half-circuit is the priciest and the only option that leaves France entirely, but delivers the most dramatic high-alpine scenery.
