⚠️ What makes this different: every night is a deluxe hotel with pool/spa — no refuge stays at all. The trade-off is that two walking days end in a different valley than the next trailhead, so Days 3–5 need short road hops (taxi or PostBus, ~€40–140 per leg est.) between hotel bases. Routes and timings are marked ~: they mirror the guided "deluxe" TMB corridor stage-for-stage but should be re-checked against current maps/trail conditions before you go. For your mid-September window: the passes are normally snow-free and September is one of the driest months of the season, but overnight frost above ~2,400 m is routine and daylight ends ~19:30 — start early, carry full layers, and check conditions daily. Snow can dust the high cols after a storm, at which point the Fenêtre d'Arpette variant (Day 4) turns serious — the classic Bovine route is the safe default. Summer-only lifts begin closing in the second half of September (the Flégère gondola's last day was 13 September 2026), so the Day 6 finale below is planned on foot — verify closure dates before relying on any lift.
Why this option: the anniversary trip where you still hike the real TMB mountain corridor — Grand Col Ferret, Champex, Col de Balme — but sleep in a 4–5-star spa hotel every single night and cross all three countries. It is the DIY version of the Summit Guides "TMB Deluxe hotel-to-hotel" tour your wife found (~€4,350 pp guided, same corridor and hotel towns, group departures, luggage trucked for you). Doing it self-guided with the same hotel beds lands roughly €2,900–3,400 pp cheaper at September shoulder rates — the main things you give up are the guides, the scheduled group transfers, and someone else doing the booking.

Anniversary planner notes (14–21 September window): book flights January–March and hotels by March–May — the September shoulder books more easily than July and rates drop 20–30%, but TMB weeks are selling out earlier every year; reserve the two starred tables (Albert 1er, La Table d'Adrien) 3–6 weeks ahead — both close for the season around 30 September; Skyway and QC Terme slots are easy in September but still book online a few days ahead; hot-air balloon only on weather-permitting days. Insurance must cover trekking above 2,500 m with helicopter-evacuation and cross-border rescue (FR/IT/CH) — most credit-card cover excludes it. After mid-September the trails empty out and the weather windows turn stable-but-cold: start early, carry full layers, and check pass conditions daily.

Complete Trip Checklist

Documents & Money

  • Canadian passport valid 6+ months past return date; no visa needed for stays under 90 days (Schengen)
  • Travel insurance with medical evacuation coverage — essential for the Mont Blanc and Pyrenees options given remote terrain
  • Currency: Euro throughout France, Italy, and (soon) most of the route; Swiss Francs if overnighting on the Swiss side of a Mont Blanc variant
  • Notify your bank/credit card of European travel dates

Connectivity

  • EU-wide eSIM (e.g. Airalo, Holafly) is simpler than roaming add-ons, especially for the Mont Blanc route crossing 3 countries
  • Offline maps downloaded in advance (Komoot, Maps.me) as rural wifi is unreliable

Packing Essentials

  • Broken-in hiking boots (not new) — the single biggest factor in trip comfort
  • Layered clothing: alpine and mountain routes swing from single digits °C at dawn to 25°C+ midday
  • Rain shell and trekking poles, especially for Mont Blanc, Pyrenees, and Brittany's exposed coastal sections
  • Power adapter: Type E/F for France (also covers most of Italy); Type J needed only if overnighting in Switzerland
  • Sun protection and a lightweight sleeping bag liner for any refuge/basic guesthouse nights

Booking Timeline

  • Flights: book 5–7 months ahead for the best summer economy fares from Montreal
  • Luxury/spa hotel rooms: 9–12 months ahead, especially July–August
  • Restaurant reservations (Michelin-starred tables): 4–8 weeks ahead minimum
